Poverty Reduction

ACERDEN implements strategic programs to alleviate poverty and create economic opportunities for rural and peri-urban communities in Nigeria. We focus on empowering households, promoting sustainable livelihoods, and fostering long-term community resilience.

Support our work

Program Overview

Poverty remains a major barrier to sustainable development in many Nigerian communities. ACERDEN’s Poverty Reduction programs aim to tackle systemic economic challenges by promoting income-generating opportunities, financial literacy, social safety nets, and inclusive community development. By combining research, community participation, and integrated interventions, our programs create lasting change and improved livelihoods.

Key Focus Areas

Income Generation & Livelihoods:

Supporting small businesses, cooperatives, and micro-enterprises.

Financial Inclusion & Literacy:

Promoting savings, loans, and budgeting skills for households.

Skills Development & Vocational Training:

Equipping youth and women with marketable skills.

Community-Based Initiatives:

Strengthening local institutions, cooperatives, and social networks.

Social Safety & Empowerment:

Facilitating access to government or NGO support programs for the most vulnerable households.

Program Implementation Approach

Community-Led Planning:

Needs assessments and participatory program design with local stakeholders

Capacity Building:

Training on entrepreneurship, financial literacy, and livelihood strategies.

Research & Innovation:

Applied research informs effective interventions and scaling strategies.

Monitoring & Evaluation:

Impact tracked via household income, employment metrics, and program feedback.

Frequently Asked Questions on ACERDEN’s poverty reduction programs

Who can benefit from ACERDEN’s poverty reduction programs?

Rural households, small businesses, women, and youth in underserved communities are eligible.

What types of income-generating activities are supported?

ACERDEN supports small businesses, cooperatives, agriculture-based enterprises, and vocational trades.

Are women and youth prioritized?

Yes. Programs are designed to empower women and youth with skills, resources, and leadership opportunities.

How does ACERDEN measure program impact?

Through household income surveys, employment metrics, cooperative performance, and program monitoring.

Can local and international organizations collaborate on this program?

Absolutely. ACERDEN works with NGOs, donor agencies, and academic institutions to expand reach and impact.

Are communities involved in program design and implementation?

Yes. Community participation ensures programs meet local needs and foster ownership.

How can donors support poverty reduction programs?

Donors can fund initiatives, provide technical support, or partner in program implementation. Details are available on the Partner With Us page.
