Program Overview

Agriculture, Nutrition & Food Security is at the core of ACERDEN's mission to ensure that every community has reliable access to sufficient, nutritious food. Our programs empower smallholder farmers with climate-smart agricultural techniques, value chain support, and household-level nutrition education. We integrate research, capacity building, and community participation to deliver long-term food security and improved nutrition outcomes.

Key Focus Areas

Climate-Smart Agriculture

Training farmers in resilient farming methods to adapt to changing climate conditions.

Smallholder Support & Value Chains

Linking farmers to markets, cooperatives, and post-harvest solutions.

Nutrition & Food Security

Promoting balanced diets, nutrition awareness, and food storage best practices.

Capacity Building & Training

Equipping local farmers with skills in sustainable farming, agribusiness, and nutrition practices.

Program Implementation Approach

Community-Led Planning

Needs assessments conducted in partnership with local farmers to ensure relevance and ownership.

Participatory Training

Hands-on workshops on crop management, livestock care, and nutrition education.

Research & Innovation

Use of applied research to inform program design and scaling strategies.

Monitoring & Evaluation

Impact measured through baseline/endline studies and continuous program monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions on Agriculture, Nutrition & Food Security.

Who can participate in ACERDEN’s agriculture programs?

Smallholder farmers, local cooperatives, and households in rural and peri-urban communities are eligible for participation.

What types of crops and livestock are supported?

Programs focus on climate-resilient crops such as maize, cassava, vegetables, and small livestock like goats and poultry.

How does ACERDEN promote nutrition within communities?

Through household nutrition education, cooking demonstrations, school feeding initiatives, and community workshops on balanced diets.

Are farmers linked to markets?

Yes. ACERDEN facilitates market access and links cooperatives to buyers, input suppliers, and post-harvest facilities.

How is the success of the program measured?

Impact is measured via household food security surveys, crop yield monitoring, and post-training evaluations.

Can international partners support this program?

Yes. ACERDEN works with donors, NGOs, and research institutions to expand program reach and effectiveness.

Does the program include women and youth?

Yes. Women and youth are prioritized in training, agribusiness support, and leadership opportunities.
